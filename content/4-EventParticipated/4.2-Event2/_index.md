# Summary Report: “Cloud Architect”

### Event Objectives

* Create an engaging learning experience through a competitive cloud architecture game show.
* Review cloud concepts and architecture-design knowledge through questions ranging from foundational to advanced levels.
* Strengthen teamwork, rapid decision-making, communication, and strategic risk management.
* Encourage learners from different internship groups to collaborate in a five-member team.

### Speakers

* **FCAJ Team:** Event organizers, moderators, and competition coordinators.

### Key Highlights

#### Competition format

The game show consisted of head-to-head matches between two teams. Each team answered questions from a question set whose difficulty increased from easy to advanced. The team with the higher score advanced to the next round. If both teams finished with the same score, Question 11 became a speed-based tie-breaker, and the faster correct response determined the winner.

#### Participation structure

The competition accepted eight teams with five members per team. Members did not need to belong to the same internship group, which encouraged participants to connect and form cross-group teams. Each person could join only one team. Because participation was limited, teams were selected randomly, with the accepted-team result announced on 19/06/2026. Selected teams were required to attend with all registered members.

#### Strategic skills

Each team could use two special skills once during the competition:

* **Minimum Risk:** Used when the team was uncertain about an answer. An incorrect answer received no point deduction, while a correct answer earned half of the normal points.
* **Star of Hope:** Used for the answer in which the team had the greatest confidence. A correct answer earned double points, while an incorrect answer caused a double-point deduction.

These skills introduced a decision-making layer beyond technical knowledge. Teams needed to evaluate confidence, question value, current score, and competitive position before activating a skill.

#### Cloud architecture knowledge

The questions covered cloud fundamentals and architecture-design knowledge. Instead of only recalling service names, participants needed to analyze requirements, identify appropriate cloud components, compare trade-offs, and respond within a limited time.

### Key Takeaways

#### Technical understanding

* Reinforced important cloud concepts by following the questions presented to the competing teams.
* Learned how service characteristics can be connected with architecture requirements by comparing the teams' answers and the results.
* Improved my ability to evaluate possible solutions and identify unsuitable choices from the technical constraints described in each question.
* Recognized that architecture questions often require balancing security, reliability, performance, and cost rather than recalling a service name.

#### Lessons from the competing teams

* Observing the teams showed that clear, concise communication was essential when five members had to agree on one answer quickly.
* Teams that listened to different opinions and combined their members' knowledge could approach difficult questions more effectively.
* The two special skills demonstrated how confidence, risk, and the current score influenced a team's strategic decisions.
* The teams' competitive spirit, mutual support, and composure under pressure made the event both engaging and instructive.

### Applying to Work

* Use a requirement-first approach before selecting an AWS service or architecture pattern.
* State assumptions and technical trade-offs clearly when discussing a solution with colleagues.
* Apply risk-based decision-making when evidence is incomplete or time is limited.
* Review incorrect answers to understand why an alternative was unsuitable, not only why the chosen answer was correct.
* Use quiz questions and architecture scenarios as practical review materials to expand technical knowledge.

### Event Experience

#### Learning through competition

As an attendee, I followed the matches and considered each question before listening to the teams' answers. The game-show format made reviewing cloud architecture more engaging than reading questions individually. As the difficulty increased, the questions encouraged me to move from recalling basic concepts to reasoning about architecture choices and trade-offs.

#### Observing the teams

Although I did not compete as a team member, watching the teams discuss and commit to their answers gave me a clear view of effective collaboration. Their active listening, quick exchange of ideas, mutual encouragement, and determination throughout the matches demonstrated how teamwork can improve performance under time pressure.

#### Learning from strategic decisions

The Minimum Risk and Star of Hope skills made confidence assessment part of the competition. Observing when teams protected their scores and when they pursued double points helped me understand how technical confidence, risk, and the competitive situation can be considered together.

#### Lessons learned

Most of my learning came from the questions given to the teams. By considering the possible answers and observing how each team reasoned, I reviewed cloud concepts in context and learned to examine constraints and trade-offs instead of memorizing isolated answers. The teams' enthusiasm, fair-play spirit, and perseverance also showed me the importance of maintaining a positive and collaborative attitude in challenging situations.
