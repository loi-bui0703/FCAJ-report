# Sharing and Feedback

### Overall Evaluation

**1. Working Environment**<br>
The FCAJ learning environment was open, collaborative, and strongly oriented toward self-learning. Weekly roadmaps, official references, community events, and peer discussion gave me enough structure to progress while still requiring personal responsibility. I especially valued an environment where questions, experiments, and failed attempts could be discussed constructively.

**2. Support from Mentor / Team Admin**<br>
Mentors and community speakers guided learners toward architecture reasoning, hands-on verification, and official AWS documentation instead of only providing ready-made answers. Administrative information and event communication were clear enough for me to organize my study plan, while the feedback I received helped me improve the quality of my technical explanations.

**3. Relevance of Work to Academic Major**<br>
The program was closely related to my Computer Science major. Computer networking appeared in VPC routing and service connectivity; operating-system concepts appeared in containers; software engineering appeared in CI/CD, testing, GitOps, and documentation; and security appeared across identity, secrets, dependencies, infrastructure, and runtime controls.

**4. Learning & Skill Development Opportunities**<br>
I improved my ability to read technical documentation, design AWS architectures, containerize applications, automate delivery, integrate DevSecOps security gates, investigate failures, monitor workloads, and document reproducible procedures. Community sessions also strengthened my note-taking, technical communication, and career-planning skills.

**5. Company Culture & Team Spirit**<br>
The program encouraged knowledge sharing and mutual support among learners, mentors, speakers, and community members. Teamwork on the DevSecOps project required clear responsibility boundaries and consistent technical interfaces, which helped me practice communication, evidence-based discussion, and collaborative troubleshooting.

**6. Internship Policies / Benefits**<br>
The most valuable benefits were access to a structured Cloud Journey roadmap, practical AWS learning materials, technical community events, mentor perspectives, and opportunities to turn theory into a complete portfolio. The program also reinforced professional habits such as weekly reporting, cost awareness, cleanup, privacy review, and accountable use of cloud resources.

---

### Additional Questions

**What did I find most satisfying during the internship?**<br>
The most satisfying result was seeing separate concepts form one working delivery path: source code, automated security checks, container image, registry, ECS Fargate deployment, report aggregation, and monitoring. Integration failures were also valuable because they forced me to understand how identity, networking, containers, and CI/CD interact.

**What should the program improve for future interns?**<br>
Short weekly technical clinics and milestone acceptance criteria would help teams validate architecture choices earlier. I would also welcome more guided failure scenarios involving IAM trust, unhealthy probes, expired credentials, missing telemetry, cost overruns, and incomplete cleanup.

**Would I recommend the program to a friend? Why?**<br>
Yes. I would recommend FCAJ to students who are prepared to read official documentation, practice consistently, explain their design decisions, and learn from unsuccessful attempts. The program is most valuable when approached as an engineering experience rather than a checklist of AWS services.

### Suggestions & Expectations

* Introduce concise evidence checklists for foundation, CI, security, deployment, observability, documentation, and cleanup milestones.
* Continue organizing community events that connect AWS services with real production decisions and career perspectives.
* Add structured document reviews covering factual accuracy, reproducibility, evidence quality, privacy, and writing quality.
* I would like to continue participating in FCAJ and AWS community activities, develop additional cloud projects, and share what I learn with future participants.
* I sincerely appreciate the FCAJ organizers, mentors, speakers, and fellow participants for creating an environment where students can test ideas and improve through feedback.
